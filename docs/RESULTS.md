# Results: what the current map suggests

## Reading the map

The current map compares each source on three provisional dimensions:

1. **Outlook** — anxious (0) to hopeful (100)
2. **Evidence basis** — speculative (0) to data-supported (100)
3. **Source reliability** — lower (0) to higher (100), displayed as marker size in the companion HTML page

These scores are comparative review aids, not measurements of authors or predictions of the future.

## Emerging patterns

- The sources do not form a simple pro-AI/anti-AI divide. Several sources are optimistic about research assistance while demanding verification, disclosure, and human responsibility.
- The sharpest empirical warning in the current set concerns learning: AI use can improve homework completion or visible performance while reducing unaided performance. That result is relevant to course and assessment design, not a proof that all educational AI use is harmful.
- Research-mathematics sources concentrate on a different problem: an abundance of generated proofs or claims may outpace community verification, understanding, attribution, and teaching.
- Humanistic sources emphasize that mathematics has purposes beyond producing answers: conceptual understanding, judgment, intellectual formation, and a shared research culture.
- The most constructive common ground is conditional adoption: use AI for explanation, exploration, routine tasks, and formal assistance; preserve independent practice and require transparent verification for consequential claims.

## Declared concern coverage

The current ledger explicitly covers education in 6 of 24 pieces and research mathematics in 13 of 24 pieces. No declared piece currently identifies jobs in mathematics as a concern. This is a coverage gap in the collection, not a conclusion about the importance of mathematical employment. The histogram on the [viewpoint map](index.html) and [`concern-coverage.csv`](../data/concern-coverage.csv) use only each entry's stated scope and notes, so that the count remains auditable.
## Departmental implications to discuss

1. Define what students must be able to do without AI, and assess it directly.
2. Permit AI uses that make reasoning visible: explanation, critique, multiple solution paths, and error analysis.
3. Require disclosure and human responsibility for AI-assisted mathematical work.
4. Treat persuasive generated proofs as drafts until independently checked or formally verified.
5. Revisit the map regularly as evidence and capabilities change.

## Limits

This repository contains a deliberately diverse, non-random set of sources. Some entries are original essays or talks; some are news reports; some are educational baselines rather than mathematics-specific work. See the source ledger and methodology before using the scores for any decision.
