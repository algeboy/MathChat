# Initial review record

## Initiating request

> Department members are sending articles with different views on AI in mathematics. Create a chart locating sources by hope–anxiety and data-driven–pontification, with attention to undergraduate and graduate students.

## Model-assisted process

1. The requester supplied links and named talks/authors.
2. The reviewer located accessible original sources, original research where possible, and reputable reporting where direct access was unavailable.
3. Each source was summarized by central claim, scope, evidence type, limitations, outlook, evidence basis, and source reliability.
4. The requester asked to add a third reliability dimension and several additional authors.

## Assessment prompt used

> For this specific source, identify its central claim about AI in mathematics or mathematics education. Distinguish empirical findings from inference, prediction, and normative claims. Score outlook from anxious (0) to hopeful (100); evidence basis from speculative (0) to data-supported (100); and source reliability from lower (0) to higher (100), based on relevant expertise, transparency, methods, primary sourcing, and relevance. State uncertainty and do not use author prestige as a substitute for evidence.

## Known limitations and corrections

- Some entries are represented by an author homepage, interview, or secondary report rather than the exact intended talk. These are marked in the source ledger and should be upgraded to primary artifacts.
- The Economist and Yahoo entries may discuss overlapping underlying research. They are retained separately only to show the difference between primary-adjacent and secondary reporting; they should not be counted as two independent findings.
- Scores are initial review judgments, not peer-reviewed assessments. The project explicitly invites revisions.
- The complete live chat transcript is not reproduced here because it contains incidental conversational material. This record preserves the task prompt, the reusable assessment prompt, decisions, sources, and limitations needed to audit the work.
